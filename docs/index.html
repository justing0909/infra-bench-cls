Infra-Bench CLS

A global, open-source benchmark for critical infrastructure classification with Earth observation foundation models.

Guthrie, Oughton, Wessels, Rice & Corley · 2026

Paper ↗ Dataset ↗ Code ↗ Cite

What it is

Earth observation foundation models are usually measured on land cover, crop type and building footprints. Critical infrastructure is harder and less studied: the assets are small, visually similar to one another, and unevenly distributed across the world. Infra-Bench CLS asks how well these models recognise them.

The task is single-label classification of infrastructure assets from satellite imagery. Asset locations come from OpenStreetMap across seven continental regions, and each one is imaged with co-located Sentinel-2 L2A optical and Sentinel-1 SAR tiles at 10 m resolution. Ten classes span four sectors, from transmission substations and solar farms to wastewater plants, airports and data centres.

We evaluate seven foundation models against a supervised ResNet-18 and a random-features floor, under two adaptation protocols and two data scales, at three seeds each. That is thirty conditions in total, every one scored on the same fixed spatial split, so training and test assets never share a spatial block.

What we found

Fine-tuning matters more than the choice of backbone. Four of the six fine-tuned foundation models beat the supervised baseline, while none of the frozen linear probes did — every one of them landed below a ResNet-18 trained from scratch. The strongest condition was DINOv3 ViT-L/16 fine-tuned on the full training set, at 57.9% macro F1 against the baseline's 39.2%.

Performance also varies sharply by class. Airports and train stations are recognised well, while distribution substations and power plants remain difficult for every model tested. The paper reports per-class, per-sector and per-region breakdowns in full.
