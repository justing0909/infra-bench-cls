Infra-Bench CLS

A global, open-source benchmark for critical infrastructure classification with Earth observation foundation models.

Justin Guthrie1, Edward Oughton1, Konrad Wessels1, Matthew Rice1, Isaac Corley2

1George Mason University 2Taylor Geospatial

What it is

Earth observation foundation models are usually measured on land cover, crop type, and building footprints. Critical infrastructure is harder and less studied. The assets are small, they look alike, and they are spread unevenly across the world. Infra-Bench CLS measures how well these models find them.

The task is single-label classification of infrastructure assets from satellite imagery. Asset locations come from OpenStreetMap across seven continental regions, each imaged with co-located Sentinel-2 L2A optical and Sentinel-1 SAR tiles at 10 m resolution. Ten classes span four sectors, from transmission substations and solar farms to wastewater plants, airports, and data centers.

Seven foundation models are scored against a supervised ResNet-18 and a random features floor, under two adaptation protocols and two data scales, at three seeds each. That gives thirty conditions, all on one fixed spatial split, so training and test assets never share a spatial block.

What we found

Fine-tuning matters more than the choice of backbone. Four of the six fine-tuned foundation models beat the supervised ResNet-18 baseline, and none of the frozen linear probes did. The best condition was DINOv3 ViT-L/16 fine-tuned on the full training set, at 57.9% macro F1 against 39.2% for the baseline.

Results also vary sharply by class. Airports and train stations score well, while distribution substations and power plants stay difficult for every model tested.
