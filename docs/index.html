Infra-Bench CLS

A global, open-source benchmark for critical infrastructure classification with Earth observation foundation models.

Justin Guthrie1, Edward Oughton1, Konrad Wessels1, Matthew Rice1, Isaac Corley2

1George Mason University 2Taylor Geospatial

Paper ↗ Dataset ↗ Code ↗ Cite

What it is

Critical infrastructure location data is often incomplete and unevenly distributed globally, especially in developing regions. Earth observation foundation models are proposed as a way to understand the built environment more efficiently, yet they remain largely untested on the facility-scale assets that underpin a range of societal and economic functions.

Infra-Bench CLS tests them on 18,756 Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 multispectral images of facility-scale assets at 10 m resolution, covering seven continents, with asset locations drawn from OpenStreetMap. It spans 13 infrastructure classes across the energy, water, transport, and telecom sectors.

Results are reported for 10 of those 13 classes. Wind farm and port terminal carry only three test images each, too few for a stable score. Water works is hard to separate from wastewater plants and storage tanks, in the imagery and in the OpenStreetMap tagging alike, so its label cannot be trusted. The 10 retained classes hold 2,689 of the 2,813 test images.

Seven foundation models are evaluated with linear probing and fine-tuning, at two training set sizes (1.0× and 0.3×) and three seeds each, against a supervised ResNet-18 and a random features floor. That gives thirty conditions, all on one fixed spatial split, so training and test assets never share a spatial block.

What we found

Fine-tuning matters more than the choice of backbone. Four of the six fine-tuned foundation models beat the supervised ResNet-18 baseline of 39.2% macro F1, and none of the frozen linear probes did. The best was DINOv3 ViT-L/16 fine-tuned on the full training set at 57.9%, a 48% relative improvement over the baseline.

Results vary widely by class. Airports (85.3%), train stations (82.1%), and data centers (77.6%) score well, while many power sector classes stay difficult, ranging from 27.5% to 46.2%.
